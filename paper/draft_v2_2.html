EMR-ACH: A Leakage-Free Multi-Domain Benchmark for
LLM Forecasting under Realistic Temporal Constraints

Ben Remez   ·   Yehudit Aperstein   ·   Alexander Apartsin

Independent Research Collective

Draft v2.2, 2026-04-27   ·   Repository: github.com/ApartsinProjects/EMR-ACH   ·   Tag: v2.1-data-ready

Abstract

Most published large-language-model (LLM) forecasting evaluations leak. Either the question's resolution date sits inside the model's training window, or the retrieved evidence articles were published after the forecasting decision should have been made. We introduce EMR-ACH (Evidence-Marshalling Retrieval with Analysis of Competing Hypotheses), a leakage-free multi-domain benchmark of Forecast Dossiers: resolved forecasting questions paired with strictly pre-event news evidence. The benchmark spans three tracks (forecasting markets, geopolitical event intensity, and S&P 500 earnings surprises) under one unified schema and a single binary primary target (Comply vs. Surprise). We enforce a 14-day forecasting horizon and a 30-day news lookback by construction, with a hard leakage filter $\text{publish\_date} \le \text{forecast\_point} = \text{resolution\_date} - 14\text{d}$ verified at publish time and at evaluation. We release the v2.1 benchmark (6,294 dossiers, 28,945 articles), an Event Timeline Dossier (ETD) extraction layer that converts articles into atomic dated facts, a curated self-contained gold subset, and a battery of nine reproducible baselines on a shared pick-only response contract. A proposed analytical framework based on Heuer's Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (ACH) is described and deferred to a method-focused follow-up.

1. Introduction

Frontier LLMs are routinely benchmarked on forecasting tasks: predicting election outcomes, geopolitical events, corporate earnings, and curated public-interest questions [1, 2, 3]. The dominant pattern is to retrieve a bundle of news articles relevant to the question, prompt the model with question and evidence, and score the model's prediction against the resolved outcome.

Two structural contaminations undermine the validity of most such evaluations.

Resolution leakage. When the question's resolution date falls within the model's training data window, the resolved outcome is, in expectation, present in the pre-training corpus. The model is no longer forecasting; it is recalling.

Article-pool leakage. Even when resolution dates post-date the training cutoff, the retrieved evidence is often constructed retrospectively. Retrieval pipelines rank articles by semantic relevance to the question without enforcing a strict publish-before-decision-time filter, admitting articles that report on the resolved outcome itself. We observed this directly in the v2.1 build of our own pipeline before introducing the leakage filter described in this paper.

The combined effect is that reported numbers are often calibrations of memorisation and post-hoc retrieval, not measurements of forecasting capability. Closing this gap requires three changes:

  1. A forecast point $t_f$ that strictly precedes the resolution date $t_r$ by a non-trivial margin: in this work, $t_r - t_f = 14$ days.
  2. An evidence pool restricted to a lookback window $[t_f - 30\text{d},\, t_f]$ with every article's publish date verified.
  3. A unified schema that admits multiple forecasting domains while preserving track-specific labels for ablation.

This paper introduces EMR-ACH, a benchmark and pipeline that operationalise these requirements end-to-end.

1.1 Contributions

  • Benchmark. 6,294 Forecast Dossiers (FDs) across three tracks (134 ForecastBench, 5,975 GDELT-CAMEO, 185 earnings) with 28,945 referenced articles. Released at v2.1-data-ready.
  • Pipeline. A reproducible 10-stage build orchestrator with leakage-filter enforcement, dangling-reference integrity check, per-cutoff snapshotting, and a horizon/lookback CLI.
  • ETD evidence layer. A three-stage Event Timeline Dossier that distils articles into atomic dated facts, deduplicates them via a date-bucketed FAISS-CPU kNN canonicalisation, and links them to FDs.
  • Gold subset. A self-contained, curated subset under each cutoff with schema, validators, and example loaders inline.
  • Baselines. Nine reproducible methods (B1-B9) plus a majority-class reference, all on a shared pick-only response contract with plurality-vote aggregation.
  • Proposed framework (deferred). An ACH-style analytical framework for hypothesis-aware retrieval and reasoning, described as future work pending the implementation port (§10).

2. Related work

Forecasting benchmarks. ForecastBench [3] aggregates resolved questions from public forecasting markets. MIRAI [1] uses GDELT 2.0 [4] for country-pair event forecasting on a fixed CAMEO root-code intensity scheme. We share GDELT as a data source with MIRAI but use a different target (binary stability/change versus three-class intensity), a different retrieval contract (strict pre-event leakage), and a different evaluation protocol (pick-only with plurality-vote aggregation). For earnings, several authors have examined LLM forecasting against short-horizon EPS surprise [5, 6]; we add a strict lookback constraint and a unified schema across domains.

Retrieval-augmented forecasting. Retrieval-augmented generation [7] is the standard scaffold for LLM forecasting. We use Self-Consistency [8], Multi-Agent Debate [9], Tree-of-Thoughts [10], and Reflexion [11] as baselines on this scaffold. Verbalised confidence [12, 13] is included in the baseline registry but deprecated under our pick-only contract; the paper compares to a majority-class reference instead.

Analysis of Competing Hypotheses. Heuer's ACH [14] provides a structured method for marshalling evidence against multiple hypotheses by scoring each evidence item's diagnosticity, that is, the degree to which it discriminates between hypotheses. ACH motivates the EMR-ACH framework (§10) but is not used as a baseline in this paper because the implementation is not yet ported off the prior MIRAI-locked codebase.

3. The Forecast Dossier framework

A Forecast Dossier (FD) is one resolved forecasting question paired with its pre-event evidence bundle. Every FD carries the same fields regardless of source track: 

FD = {
  id, benchmark, source,
  question, background,
  hypothesis_set,                    // e.g. ["Comply", "Surprise"]
  hypothesis_definitions,
  ground_truth,                      // ∈ hypothesis_set
  ground_truth_idx,                  // ∈ {0, …, |hypothesis_set|-1}
  forecast_point,                    // UTC timestamp t_f
  resolution_date,                   // UTC timestamp t_r
  default_horizon_days,              // = 14 in v2.2
  lookback_days,                     // = 30 in v2.2
  article_ids,                       // pointers into articles.jsonl
  prior_state_30d, prior_state_stability, prior_state_n_events,
  fd_type,                           // ∈ {stability, change, unknown}
  metadata                           // track-specific (ticker, actors, …)
}

The FD framework enforces two invariants that together close the leakage gap:

Horizon invariant. $t_r - t_f = h$ where $h$ is the horizon (default 14 days). This is enforced at FD construction and re-asserted at gold subset filtering.

Leakage invariant. For every $a \in \texttt{article\_ids}$, the corresponding article's publish_date satisfies $\text{publish\_date}(a) \le t_f$. Verified at publish time (the orchestrator dumps a meta/dangling_article_ids.txt for any survivor that violates) and re-asserted at evaluation.

The primary forecasting target across all three tracks is the binary label Comply vs. Surprise: did the prior expectation hold, or did something break? Track-specific multi-class labels (Beat/Meet/Miss for earnings, Peace/Tension/Violence for GDELT-CAMEO) are preserved as metadata.x_multiclass_* fields for ablation. The fd_type stratifier splits FDs into stability (prior expectation holds) and change (prior breaks); paper-grade evaluation reports both overall accuracy and the change subset.

4.3 Leakage controls

The pipeline enforces three layers of leakage protection:

  • Pre-fetch: every per-track fetcher (fetch_gdelt_cameo_news.py, fetch_earnings_news.py, ForecastBench loaders) accepts the FD's forecast_point as a cutoff and rejects any record dated after it.
  • At-link: the relational link_earnings_articles.py assembles per-FD article lists by joining ticker × date; the date filter is applied locally even when the upstream source returns out-of-window records.
  • At-publish: step_publish computes the dangling-reference set (FD article_ids that do not resolve to any article in the unified pool) and writes them to meta/dangling_article_ids.txt. A non-zero count is treated as a blocker for tagging.

4.4 Two strategies for the v2.2 article pool

v2.2 introduces a 14-day horizon and 30-day lookback, redefining $t_f = t_r - 14\text{d}$. Two parallel article-pool strategies are explored:

Reuse-first (§6.1A in the spec). Take the v2.1 published articles, apply the leakage filter at the new $t_f$, drop FDs whose article list is empty after filtering. Fast (under one hour) but loses coverage where the v2.1 retrieval was biased toward articles dated near the resolution date. Yields 89 FDs for forecastbench and earnings combined.

CC-News rebuild (§6.1B in the spec). Fetch the entire Common Crawl News archive [15] for the 30-day lookback window, index it, and re-query per FD against the new pool. Slower (a 30-day month is roughly 600 GB of compressed WARC shards), but yields a properly diverse pool with publish dates extracted by trafilatura at fetch time.

5. Event Timeline Dossier (ETD)

Articles are noisy units of evidence. A single high-impact event is reported by dozens of outlets within hours, often with overlapping but not identical content. Reasoning over articles directly forces the model to spend tokens on cross-article deduplication that should have happened upstream.

The Event Timeline Dossier (ETD) is a three-stage pipeline that distils articles into atomic dated facts:

StageScriptOutputMethod
1. Extractarticles_to_facts.pyraw factsgpt-4o-mini Batch API
2. Dedupetd_dedup.pycanonical factsOpenAI embeddings + date-bucketed FAISS-CPU IndexFlatIP, $\tau = 0.92$
3. Linketd_link.pyFD-linked factsper-cutoff primary_article_id resolution
Filteretd_filter.pyproduction factsblocklist + medium+ confidence + asserted polarity + no-future + linked

Stage 2 deduplication exploits a key structural property of the dataset: facts about the same event cluster temporally. We bucket facts by ISO date and search for near-duplicates only within a $\pm 3$-day window per query, replacing the brute-force $O(N^2)$ matmul with a date-bucketed search that runs in roughly $O(N \cdot B)$ where $B$ is the mean bucket size. For 78,630 facts spread over 365 days, this drops dedup wall-clock from ~15 minutes to ~8 seconds, with exact recall within the window constraint.

6. Gold subset

The published benchmark is large and heterogeneous; the gold subset is a small, curated, defensible slice intended for headline evaluations. The build script (scripts/build_gold_subset.py) applies a filter cascade and stratified sampling:

  1. Article count $\geq$ min_articles.
  2. Distinct article publish-dates $\geq$ min_distinct_days (no single-news-cycle FDs).
  3. Average article text length $\geq$ min_avg_chars (no title-only stubs).
  4. Source diversity $\geq$ min_source_diversity distinct domains.
  5. Per-track predictability filters (FB topic denylist for sports/lottery; GDELT-CAMEO actor-pair geopolitical-significance set; earnings restricted to S&P 100).
  6. Stratified sampling toward per-(benchmark, fd_type) quotas.

The output folder is fully self-contained: forecasts.jsonl + articles.jsonl + facts.jsonl + schema/ + examples/ + build_manifest.json + checksums.sha256 + LICENSE + CITATION.cff. A consumer can download the gold folder and use it without any other file from the repository.

7. Baseline battery

7.1 Pick-only response contract

Every baseline returns one hypothesis label per FD:

{"prediction": "<one of hypothesis_set>", "reasoning": "..."}

Multi-sample methods aggregate via plurality vote; ties are broken by hypothesis_set order. We deliberately drop probability-distribution outputs for two reasons. First, calibration is a separate, well-studied confound that should not be tangled with the forecasting question. Second, plurality-vote on argmax is what users actually do at decision time, so it is the more honest measurement.

9. Discussion and limitations

9.1 What this benchmark measures, and what it does not

EMR-ACH measures forecasting accuracy under a strict horizon and lookback contract. It does not measure calibration (probability outputs were dropped on purpose). It does not measure long-horizon reasoning beyond 14 days. It does not measure the model's ability to gather evidence: every method receives the same retrieved bundle.

9.2 Track imbalance

The v2.1 composition is dominated by GDELT-CAMEO (5,975 of 6,294 FDs, 95%). Within GDELT-CAMEO, the binary primary target is itself imbalanced toward the Comply class (74%, equivalent to the country pair maintaining its prior interaction pattern). This compresses the score gap between methods on the overall accuracy measurement; the change-subset breakdown in Table 3 is therefore the headline number, with overall accuracy reported as a secondary check. We address the GDELT-CAMEO skew in v2.2 via fd_type stratification at sampling time and via a planned actor-pair rebalancer (backlog item H3).

9.3 Article-pool coverage gaps

Common Crawl News, while large, has uneven coverage of paywalled US-finance editorial outlets (Bloomberg, WSJ, Financial Times, Reuters). The v2.2 CC-News build documents this transparently; the alternative reuse-first build supplements with v2.1's NYT, Guardian, Google News, and earnings-specific (Finnhub, EDGAR) sources. We treat the full source mix as a v2.2 design choice, not a clean ablation.

9.4 Ethical considerations

The benchmark is built from publicly hosted news sources under their respective terms (GDELT public KG, ForecastBench resolved questions, NYT and Guardian APIs with appropriate rate limits, Common Crawl CC-NEWS public mirror, Yahoo Finance and Finnhub for earnings metadata). We redistribute schemas, FD pointers, and trafilatura-extracted excerpts in the gold subset; full article text in the larger benchmark is referenced by URL with provenance recorded. Users are responsible for respecting upstream licences when scaling the pipeline. No personally identifiable information is introduced by our processing.

10. Future work: the EMR-ACH analytical framework

The intended end state of this line of work is a method that combines four ACH-inspired components on top of the benchmark introduced here:

  1. Contrastive indicators. Per-(article, hypothesis) score for support and disconfirm via a single LLM call, normalised across hypotheses with log-odds. Generalised to arbitrary hypothesis_set rather than the legacy MIRAI 4-CAMEO scheme.
  2. Hybrid retrieval. MMR diversity rerank + Reciprocal Rank Fusion of dense and BM25 signals + temporal decay $\propto e^{-\alpha (t_f - t_a)}$ with $\alpha$ tunable.
  3. Diagnosticity weighting. Heuer's analysis-matrix-A: rows = evidence items, columns = hypotheses, cells = $d_{ij} = (s_{ij} - \bar{s}_{i\cdot}) \cdot \mathrm{Var}_{h}(s_{ih})$ where $s_{ij}$ is item $i$'s support for hypothesis $j$. Aggregation across $i$ produces per-hypothesis scores.
  4. Multi-round multi-agent debate. $N$-round argumentation per hypothesis with the diagnosticity matrix as a shared scratchpad; judge picks at the end.

The implementation in src/pipeline/ is partial today. docs/EMRACH_IMPLEMENTATION_AUDIT.md traces each component to a SHIPPED, PARTIAL, or MISSING verdict. The ported, GPU-free, FD-aware version is filed as backlog item H7 (P0) and is intended to be the headline contribution of a follow-up paper. We deliberately scope the present work to the benchmark and baselines so that the method's evaluation surface is well-characterised before the method itself is ported.

11. Conclusion

EMR-ACH is the benchmark we wanted to use but could not find: multi-domain, leakage-free by construction, with a non-trivial forecasting horizon, a unified binary primary target, and a curated self-contained gold subset. We release the v2.1 data, the v2.2 horizon-corrected build pipeline, the ETD evidence layer, and a battery of nine reproducible baselines on a shared pick-only contract. The proposed analytical framework that motivates the project name is documented in detail and deferred to a follow-up paper, on the principle that benchmark contributions should be evaluable independently of the methods they enable.

Empirical compute footprint

The full benchmark build, ETD post-publish chain, gold subset construction, and one Batch-API baselines run cost approximately:

  • Article fetch (per-track + Common Crawl): network and disk only.
  • Relevance embeddings (text-embedding-3-small, Batch API): ≈ $0.30 per 78k facts.
  • ETD Stage-1 extraction (gpt-4o-mini, Batch API): ≈ $5 per 30k articles.
  • Baseline battery on 80-FD gold subset: ≈ $0.30 across all 10 methods.
  • Total reproducibility budget: under $10 per cutoff-month.

G8: Date-bucketed FAISS kNN for ETD Stage-2 dedup

Naive Stage-2 dedup runs an $O(N^2)$ pairwise cosine matmul. We replace this with a date-bucketed search that exploits the dedup-window constraint: facts more than $w$ days apart cannot cluster regardless of similarity. Algorithm:

group facts by ISO date d
for each day d:
  bucket = ⋃ {facts on d', |d - d'| ≤ w}
  index  = FAISS.IndexFlatIP(D); index.add(embeddings(bucket))
  query  = embeddings(facts on d)
  pairs  = index.search(query, top_k+1) ∩ {threshold ≥ τ, j ≠ i}
emit deduplicated pairs

For 78,630 facts, $w = 3$, $\tau = 0.92$, $\text{top\_k} = 10$: brute-force runs in ~15 minutes; G8 runs in ~8 seconds with exact recall under the window constraint. Parity tests in tests/test_etd_dedup_knn.py verify pair-set equality with brute force when $w$ is wide enough to span the corpus.

OpenAI Batch API operational notes

The pipeline relies on the OpenAI Batch API for embeddings (relevance), Stage-1 extraction (ETD), and baselines. We hit and engineered around three concrete failure modes:

  • 50,000-request per-batch cap. Auto-chunking in src/common/openai_embeddings.py:encode_batch splits requests above the cap into parallel sub-batches; round-robin polling collects them.
  • Long-tail stalls. An observed batch stalled at 49,996 / 50,000 in the cancelling state for over an hour. We added a salvage path: if the batch terminates in cancelled with a populated output_file_id, the partial output is downloaded and missing custom_ids are padded via sync calls.
  • Locally-completed shards. For repeat or alternate-key processing, a --workers 1 sync fallback can pre-populate output.jsonl on disk; encode_batch Phase 2 detects this and short-circuits the OpenAI poll entirely.
